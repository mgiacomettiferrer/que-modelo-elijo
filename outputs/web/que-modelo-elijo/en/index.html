1 · What it is for

Which model should my agent use?

If you are building an agent, you have to choose the language model that runs it. There are dozens, and they all claim to be good. This page helps you decide in two steps: first, understand how the market lines up; then, if you want, find the model that fits your task and your budget.

There is no “best model”

The one that wins at writing code is not necessarily the one that wins at reading documents. It depends on the task and on how much you can pay.

Capability is expensive

Between the most basic tier and the most capable one, reasoning capability grows 1.9 times and the input price grows 10 times. Doubling capability costs five times more.

“Fast” means three things

How long it takes to start, how many tokens it writes per second, and how long it takes to finish a task. A model can write fast and still take minutes to begin.

2 · How the market lines up

The map of the 24 models

Each point is a model. What matters is where it sits:

  • To the right (PC1): more capable. One axis summarizes four quality tests: reasoning, coding, agentic work and documents.
  • Up (PC2): writes more tokens per second once it has started. It is not response time: a model near the top can take minutes to begin.
  • Color and shape: the tier. Size: how much it costs. Bigger means more expensive (estimated weighted cost per million tokens).

Hover over a point to see its data; click it to see its profile.

PC1 and PC2 are principal components, not variables: PC1 explains 70.1% of the variance and PC2 13.6%. Latency was left out of the model. Source: artificialanalysis.ai/models, cutoff 2026-10-19.

Model profile

Four tiers of the same ladder

We group the models into four tiers so we can talk about them. They are not four kinds of model: they are four cuts of a continuous ladder where capability rises, speed falls and price follows. The split is not validated: nobody tested whether it holds when the data changes.

What this page cannot tell you

  • It does not represent the whole market. These are 24 models from 16 providers; 7 are from Anthropic and OpenAI and 1 from Google. The frontier tier has only two providers, so Claude will show up at the top often.
  • The tiers are not validated. They come from a single clustering run; no stability analysis was performed.
  • A single host. Everything comes from Artificial Analysis. These scores are not comparable with another leaderboard's, even when the test has the same name.
  • Tasks without data. Instruction following (IFBench) has no data at all; multimodal has 13 of 24 and data analysis 16 of 24.
  • The weighted cost is not the cost per task. The weights are shares of tokens; the real cost depends on how many tokens each task consumes.
